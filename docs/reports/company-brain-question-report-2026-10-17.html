What the run showed

Detailed project lookup is unreliable

In 22 questions about projects the caller was allowed to see, Company Brain said it could not find the project. Portfolio briefs and task lists could still return those same projects. The other named “not found” reply was a correct refusal when Vikram asked for Maya’s Willow project.

Approved absence data crosses role boundaries

Both the architect and employee received the same company-wide leave and WFH list when they asked about next week. HR can see approved availability, but the employee and architect prompts were not limited to their own project teams.

Several role boundaries worked

Leads received approvals for their own direct reports; HR was denied the pending approval queue; Vikram was denied Maya’s project; Anika’s company-wide portfolio request returned only her two permitted projects; Dev declined another employee’s leave balance and salary details.

Some answers stop before finishing

3 answers end mid-list, including a workload comparison and two architecture recommendations. One HR reporting-structure question returned a generic completion error.

Seeded data limits are usually stated

Company Brain correctly said contract values, revenue, compensation, birthdays, project documents, stack details, and service endpoints were not present in the retrieved records. HR also noted that active employment status was not available.

HR reporting needs a consistency pass

A directory answer listed only Dev as reporting to Aditi, while another HR answer listed Aditi’s five direct reports. The HR weekly brief also questioned approval status for leave entries that other availability answers called approved.
